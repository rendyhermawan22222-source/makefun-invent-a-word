Build task: invent one brand-new English word for make.fun's "Invent a Word We Need"
bounty and define it in a single clear line of at most 140 characters.

The word must name something real that English has no word for — a small,
everyday moment a reader recognises at once — and it must sound like English
could have grown it: sayable, spellable, memorable, built from familiar roots.
The definition must be exact and self-contained, landing in one reading, with
its last clause carrying the turn.

I brainstormed 16 candidate words and definitions (wave-back-at-the-wrong-person,
rehearsing a phone call, the goodbye-then-same-direction walk, opening the fridge
just to look, forgetting a name mid-conversation, tab hoarding, and more), checked
the leading candidates against the web to make sure none already exists, and
picked the strongest: "Wavemiss" — the wave you give a stranger who was waving
at the person behind you. It is 76 characters, plain ASCII, one line, and the
turn sits in the final clause.

The entry program (main.py) writes that one line to /output/entry.txt, reads it
back, and verifies it is exactly one line, 1-140 characters, non-blank ASCII.
It is deterministic, uses no network and no randomness.
